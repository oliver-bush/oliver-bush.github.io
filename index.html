Oliver Bush

Economist working at the intersection of macroeconomics, financial economics and economic history

Postdoctoral Fellow in Long-Term Fiscal Policy, National Bureau of Economic Research (2027) Visiting Lecturer, London School of Economics Senior Advisor, Financial Stability, Bank of England

My research uses historical narratives, archival evidence and modern empirical methods to study how policy regimes shape inflation, credit and public-debt dynamics, with a focus on monetary–fiscal interactions, financial stability and central banking.

In 2027 I will be a Postdoctoral Fellow in Long-Term Fiscal Policy at the National Bureau of Economic Research. I completed a PhD in Economic History at the London School of Economics in 2024. I am a Senior Advisor for Financial Stability at the Bank of England, where I have worked in financial stability and monetary analysis since 2009, and I was a Research Fellow at the Bank for International Settlements in 2026.

Research programme

Monetary and macroprudential policy

How interest rates and quantitative credit controls act on inflation, bank lending and output, and whether the two instruments are substitutes.

Fiscal regimes and inflation

How governments finance fiscal expansions, when debt is expected to be backed by future surpluses, and what happens to prices when that expectation breaks.

Financial stability and public policy

Analytical frameworks for financial policy, macroprudential indicators, bank capital requirements, sovereign debt and the international monetary system.
